3. What we collect about account holders

Account and sign-in

  • Your email address. It is how you sign in and is the identifier of your account, stored as you typed it (lower-cased). We have no passwords.
  • Sign-in links. Each link carries a random token. We store only a SHA-256 hash of it, together with your email address and the page to return you to. A link works once and expires after 15 minutes. The stored row is removed when the link is used; a link that is never clicked stays stored until your account is deleted.
  • Sign-in sessions and tokens. Signing in creates a session record and the tokens our dashboard uses to act for you (15-minute access tokens, 30-day refresh tokens; the dashboard's own session lasts 7 days). They are kept until your account is deleted.
  • A cookie that ties your browser to that session. See section 9.

Accounts and teams

  • The accounts you belong to, your role in each, and the apps each account owns, with their hostnames and plan.
  • Team invitations: the invited address, who sent the invitation, and when. Invitations expire after 7 days.
  • Team invitations and sign-in links are sent by email; see section 7.

Billing

Paid plans are not yet available, and no payment provider holds any data for us today. When they are available:

  • Card details go from your browser directly to Stripe. They never pass through our servers and we never see them.
  • We send Stripe your account's email address and an internal account id, and keep Stripe's customer and subscription ids, your subscription's status and period, and card fingerprints Stripe returns, which we use only to block card-testing fraud.

Your requests to our own dashboard and sign-in pages

Our dashboard and sign-in service are themselves apps running on rewind.js, so your requests to them are recorded exactly as section 4 describes — including the email address you type into the sign-in form. We use these records to operate, debug, and secure those services.

Operational telemetry

We collect metrics and service logs to keep the platform running — request and error rates, latency, storage use, cluster health. These carry your apps' identifiers and hostnames, including custom domains. They do not carry request or response bodies.

Our websites

rewindjs.com and docs.rewindjs.com are static pages. We do not run analytics on them and set no cookies there. Until we finish removing them, some pages load fonts or icons from third parties, who receive your IP address and browser details when they do; see section 7.

4. What the platform records about traffic to apps

If you run an app on rewind.js, this is what we store about requests your users send it. You are the controller of that data and decide what you tell your users; our DECISION: DPA link sets out our obligations to you.

Request bodies are recorded by design. Anything your users send in a request body, and anything your handler fetches from another service, is stored so the request can be replayed. Do not send secrets or special-category data through a rewind.js app unless you intend it to be recorded.

For each request we record:

  • the method, host, and path including the query string, the response status, timing, the handler's console output and any exception, and any tags your code attaches;
  • the names of every request header, and the values of the headers your handler reads — reading cookies records the whole Cookie header;
  • the request body. It is stored before your handler runs, so that the request is durable. If the handler never reads it, the record drops its reference, but the stored bytes are not removed;
  • the values your handler reads from its key-value store, and the keys it writes;
  • the responses — headers and bodies — to any outbound HTTP requests your handler makes.

We do not record your app's response bodies.

Client IP addresses

Your handler sees a masked client address by default: IPv4 addresses have the final octet zeroed, and IPv6 addresses are cut to their first 48 bits. The address is recorded only if your handler reads it. A handler can ask for the full address through unmaskedIp(), a capability it is handed deliberately; when it does, the full address is recorded with that request. That call is the app operator's decision, and theirs to disclose.

What is encrypted

Recorded payloads — request bodies, outbound responses and message contents — are encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a key belonging to the app, or under a per-person key the app names (see section 6). So is the replicated log every write passes through. Other things are stored as written:

  • the record fields above other than payloads — paths and query strings, header values, console output, tags;
  • key-value store contents, unless the app wrote them under a per-person key, and all key names;
  • deployed code and static files.

6. How long we keep it

Request records

Each plan has a retention window — 7 days on the free plan, 30 on pro, 365 on enterprise. A customer may choose a shorter one.

The window limits what can be read, not yet what is stored. Records older than the window are hidden from every way of reading them — the dashboard, replay, the CLI, and the APIs. Automatic physical deletion past the window is not built yet, so hidden records remain in our storage, where our operators can still reach them. Ask us at privacy@loop46.com if you need records removed sooner.

Erasing one person's data

An app can tie a request to a person with shredKey(id); that request's recorded payloads and the key-value values it writes are then encrypted under that person's own key. shredKey.destroy(id) destroys the key on every server, permanently, and those payloads and values become unreadable everywhere they are stored. It does not reach key names, tags, paths and query strings, or recorded header values, so apps should keep identifying details out of those.

Account data

Kept while your account is open. When you delete your account, see section 8 for what goes and what remains.

8. Your rights, export, and deleting your account

You can ask us for a copy of your data, to correct it, to delete it, to restrict or object to how we use it, or to send it elsewhere. Depending on where you live, some of these are legal rights; we extend them to every account holder.

Self-service

  • Export. The dashboard exports your account — members and roles, pending invitations, apps, and billing references — and, per app, its key-value data and code.
  • Deletion. Deleting your account from the dashboard is immediate and cannot be undone. If you are the only owner of a team, transfer or delete that team first. Deletion takes you out of every team, cancels any subscription, deletes each app your account owns, and removes your account, sign-in and session records.

What deleting an account does to app data

Each deleted app's encryption key is destroyed on every server, so its recorded payloads become unreadable wherever they are stored. Its code, static files, data stores and exports are deleted. What remains:

  • the unencrypted fields of its request records (section 4) — hidden from every reading surface, and in storage until automatic deletion past the retention window is built;
  • a record that the deletion happened, including the account's email address, kept DECISION: indefinitely, or for N years as evidence of the deletion;
  • the records of your own requests to our dashboard and sign-in pages (section 3), which are hidden after 365 days and, like all request records, not yet deleted;
  • if you were a paying customer, your customer record at Stripe: the subscription is cancelled, but the customer record is DECISION: kept for N years for tax records, or deleted.

Anything else

For anything the dashboard does not do — including a copy of request records or sign-in data — email privacy@loop46.com. We acknowledge within DECISION: 5 working days and complete within DECISION: 30 days. We may need to confirm that the request comes from the account's email address.

If you are in the EEA or the UK, you may also complain to your local data protection authority.

11. Security

What we do: TLS on every connection from the internet. Each app's data lives in its own replicated store under its own storage prefix and its own encryption key. Handlers run under per-request time and memory limits, and cannot reach private network addresses. Client IPs are masked by default. Sign-in link tokens are stored hashed.

What we do not do, stated plainly: the items listed in section 4 as stored as written are not encrypted by us, and we do not ask our storage provider to encrypt them either. If your app handles data that must be encrypted at rest throughout, keep it in payloads under shredKey, or do not put it on rewind.js yet.

To report a vulnerability, write to security@loop46.com.
