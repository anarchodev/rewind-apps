Security disclosure policy

Effective DECISION: publication date

rewind.js runs untrusted JavaScript from many customers on shared machines. If you have found a way to break that — or anything else that puts our customers or their users at risk — we want to hear about it privately, and we will work with you on fixing and disclosing it.

Reporting

Write to security@loop46.com (Loop46, Inc. is the company that operates rewind.js) DECISION: also GitHub private reporting on anarchodev/rove. Please do not open a public issue.

Tell us what you found, how to reproduce it, what an attacker gains, and whether you accessed anyone else's data along the way. A proof of concept against your own app is ideal.

What to expect

  • We acknowledge within 2 working days.
  • We tell you within DECISION: 7 days whether we accept the report and how severe we think it is.
  • We aim to fix critical issues in production within DECISION: 7 days and others within DECISION: 90 days, and keep you updated.
  • We coordinate disclosure with you: by default we publish an advisory once the fix is deployed, and no later than 90 days after your report unless we agree otherwise.
  • We do not run a bug bounty and cannot pay for reports. We will credit you if you would like.

Scope

Most wanted — anything that crosses a tenant boundary:

  • reading or altering another customer's code, data, request records or replays;
  • escaping the JavaScript sandbox or its per-request time and memory limits;
  • reaching the control plane, another customer's storage, or internal network addresses from a handler;
  • reading data after its key has been destroyed with shredKey.destroy or account deletion;
  • flaws in sign-in, the dashboard, or the platform session cookie.

In scope: rewindjs.com and its subdomains, apps served under rewindjs.app (tested through your own app), and the open-source engine at github.com/anarchodev/rove.

Out of scope: volumetric denial of service; rate limits or resource caps on the free plan; physical attacks or social engineering of our staff; bugs in a customer's own app code; issues in third-party services we use (report those to them); missing hardening headers or version banners without a demonstrated impact; scanner output without a working proof.

Rules for testing

  • Test only against accounts and apps you own. Signing up is free.
  • Do not access, change or keep other people's data. If you reach any, stop, tell us, and delete what you hold.
  • No denial of service, no spam through outbound requests or email, and nothing that degrades the service for others.
  • Keep the details confidential until we have agreed on disclosure.

Safe harbour

DECISION: wording — counsel to review If you research and report in good faith and follow this policy, we will not pursue or support legal action against you for it, including under anti-hacking or anti-circumvention laws or our Terms of Service, and we will make that known if a third party takes action against you over it. If you are unsure whether something is allowed, ask us first.
